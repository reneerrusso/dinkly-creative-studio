# DINKLY Brand Integrations

## Principle

A brand integration should feel like part of an ordinary DINKLY moment, not an advertisement. The relationship remains the story. The product replaces a prop the scene already needs.

## Approved categories

- Coffee
- Snacks
- Skincare
- Books
- Flowers
- Games
- Drinks
- Groceries
- Blankets
- Home goods
- Travel accessories

## Integration test

Approve an opportunity only when:

1. The unbranded storyline works emotionally.
2. The product has a natural functional role.
3. Removing the logo would leave the story intact.
4. Packaging does not require a busier composition.
5. The product can remain secondary to Dinka and Dinko.

## Reference separation

When character and product references are both present, label them explicitly:

- Character reference: identity, anatomy, markings, proportions, and style only.
- Product reference: product shape, colors, package structure, and logo only.

Do not allow the product artwork, photography, mascot, rendering style, or color treatment to influence the DINKLY characters.

## Placeholder-first workflow

Use placeholder-first production when packaging detail could damage character accuracy:

1. Generate and approve the emotional scene with a simple one-color placeholder product.
2. Lock character identity, anatomy, composition, background, and text.
3. Edit only the placeholder region using the product reference.
4. Re-run character and product QA.

This is the default for complex snack bags, bottles, skincare packaging, board games, and objects with small text.

## Scale guidance

Describe products relative to the character or canvas. Avoid `tiny`, `normal-sized`, and `human-sized`.

- Phone: approximately the size of Dinko's face and no more than 8–10% of the canvas.
- Mug: fits naturally between the nub arms and body without hiding the mouth.
- Snack bag: below shoulder height and no wider than one-third of a character body.
- Book: narrow enough for the eyes and bow or hair tufts to remain visible.

## Campaign and evergreen versions

For every sponsored execution, consider producing:

- Campaign version with accurate product and approved marks
- Evergreen version with generic packaging and no protected campaign copy

Confirm usage rights, approval requirements, territories, term, and required disclosures outside the image-production prompt.

## Avoid

- Hero packaging larger than the characters
- Product-only compositions without an emotional story
- Multiple packages added only for visibility
- Characters presenting products with human hands
- Logos on clothing or body surfaces
- Product reference style bleeding into character art
- Claims or benefits not supplied by the partner
- Invented product details

## Brand-placement brief

Every integration brief should record:

- Unbranded emotional concept
- Natural product role
- Product reference source
- Required package details
- Details allowed to simplify
- Relative scale
- Placement and character interaction
- Placeholder-first decision
- Character risks
- Product risks
- Evergreen alternative
- Final approval owner
