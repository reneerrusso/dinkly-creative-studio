# DINKLY Viral Framework

## Purpose

This framework evaluates whether a concept is likely to earn attention and sharing while remaining unmistakably DINKLY. It provides directional creative judgment, not a promise of performance.

Social-performance data may strengthen or weaken a recommendation. Never invent metrics, claim causation from correlation, or present a hypothesis as a measured fact.

## Primary formula

The proven comparison format is:

`X` / `X WITH YOU`

The activity remains nearly identical. The left side feels ordinary, neutral, bored, or gently sad. The right side feels warmer because the characters are together.

The left side is never devastating. The right side is never unrealistically perfect.

## Approved adjacent formats

- `X BEFORE YOU` / `X AFTER YOU`
- Short dialogue series
- Favorite-person series
- Single emotional panels
- Close-up affection panels
- Dark cartoon storybook environments with white handwritten text
- Brand-integrated everyday moments

## The one-second test

A strong concept communicates, in order:

1. Familiar activity
2. Emotional contrast
3. Relationship warmth
4. DINKLY identity

If the concept needs explanation, multiple speech bubbles, or a detailed setting before the emotion lands, it is not ready.

## Shareability triggers

Treat these as hypotheses until social data supports them:

- A routine couples immediately recognize
- A gesture people want to send to their partner
- A title pair that reads like a personal message
- Physical closeness that is comfortable rather than performative
- A scene that says something specific with very little text
- A relationship truth that feels achievable

## Save triggers

Potential save triggers include:

- Frame-worthy composition
- Seasonal relevance
- A useful expression of affection
- A comforting reminder
- A repeatable ritual
- A giftable or greeting-card-ready image

## Comment triggers

Potential comment triggers include:

- Recognition of a shared habit
- A gentle difference in how couples perform the same routine
- A favorite-person prompt that invites tagging
- A scene with a specific but widely relatable detail

Avoid engagement bait and forced questions.

## Concept scoring rubric

Score each criterion from 1–10:

- Emotional clarity
- Relatability
- Visual simplicity
- Scroll-stopping potential
- Shareability
- Save potential
- Comment potential
- DINKLY brand fit
- Originality
- Brand-integration potential
- Ease of Nano Banana execution
- Risk of character distortion, where 1 is low risk and 10 is high risk

Scores are directional. The total uses the inverse of distortion risk so safer concepts are rewarded.

## Improvement rule

Do not automatically reject a concept because one criterion is below 9. Identify the weakest criterion, state why it is weak, and revise the concept once before final output.

Typical improvements:

- Remove a secondary action
- Replace a generic setting with a recognizable routine
- Reduce prop types
- Strengthen the physical closeness in the right scene
- Shorten the title
- Change the camera angle to improve readability
- Replace a repeated setting with an adjacent emotional angle
- Move product accuracy to a second edit pass

## Originality without drift

Generate concepts adjacent to proven winners, not copies of them. Avoid repeating couch, coffee, bed, shopping-cart, and movie scenes unless the emotional insight, camera language, seasonal context, or act of care is meaningfully different.

Originality must come from a fresh human truth, not from visual complexity.

## Evidence standard

Always label claims as one of:

- **Measured fact:** supplied metric or calculated rate.
- **Observed trait:** visible element in the uploaded creative.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible explanation supported by evidence but not proven.
- **Unverified assumption:** idea requiring more data.

High-confidence learning requires multiple strong examples or a very large and well-matched performance gap. One viral post can justify exploration, not a permanent rule.
