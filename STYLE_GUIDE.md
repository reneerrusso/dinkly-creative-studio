# DINKLY Style Guide

## Visual character

Official DINKLY artwork is flat, 2D, vector-based, matte, rounded, clean, minimal, modern, wholesome, and immediately readable.

The characters must remain the focal point. The environment establishes the ordinary moment and then gets out of the way.

## Required visual language

- Thick, clean black outlines
- Flat solid colors
- Rounded character-safe furniture
- Simple rounded props
- Large negative space
- Minimal compositions
- Soft pastel backgrounds
- Clear silhouettes
- One readable action per character

## Prohibited visual language

- Photorealism
- Painterly rendering
- 3D rendering
- Complex textures
- Realistic architecture
- Dramatic shadows
- Heavy lighting effects
- Dense scenery
- Random decorative clutter
- Neon or heavily saturated palettes
- Perspective that distorts character size

## Background system

Split comics use one continuous background fill across the entire canvas. The two sides must not receive separate shades, lighting treatments, or color casts unless a brief explicitly requests that exception.

Approved background families:

- Warm cream
- Pastel peach
- Dusty blue
- Soft lavender
- Mint
- Warm sage
- Butter yellow
- Dusty rose
- Warm sand
- Soft coral
- Powder blue
- Pistachio
- Blush pink

Each comic generally uses one pastel background, one primary accent color, warm neutrals, and a small number of scene-setting props.

Do not default to white. Use white only when the brief explicitly requests a white-background minimalist comic.

## Split-comic layout

- Square 1:1 canvas
- Equal left and right narrative zones
- One continuous pastel background
- Thin black vertical divider at the exact center when required by the current prompt brief
- Divider approximately half the canvas height and vertically centered
- Bottom caption area protected from characters and props
- Matching camera logic and environment across both sides

## Typography

Current production typography uses Bubblebody Neue Regular unless a brief explicitly selects the approved dark-storybook treatment.

- Place split-comic captions at the bottom.
- Center each caption in its half.
- Use solid black text on pastel backgrounds.
- Keep both captions on the same baseline and at the same weight.
- Render only the supplied text.
- Do not introduce quotation marks, extra punctuation, shadows, outlines, or replacement fonts.

Dark storybook comics may use white handwritten text only when the concept is explicitly approved for that format.

## Prop and furniture palette

Build a recognizable minimalist scene with three to five relevant prop types. Prefer functional objects such as tables, chairs, coffee machines, beds, sofas, counters, laundry baskets, carts, books, lamps, or blankets over scattered decorative symbols.

Each prop should use one flat fill color plus an optional black outline. Use no more than one restrained accent color. Furniture must support the scene without dwarfing or hiding the characters.

## Camera rotation

Rotate framing across the content calendar to prevent visual repetition:

- Close-up
- Medium
- Wide
- Profile
- Overhead
- Over-the-shoulder

Use the simplest angle that communicates the action while protecting the character model. Avoid complex perspective for its own sake.

## Readability test

At thumbnail size, a viewer should identify:

1. The ordinary activity
2. Who is alone and who is together
3. The emotional contrast
4. The characters' locked identities

If any point requires zooming or reading a long caption, simplify the scene.
